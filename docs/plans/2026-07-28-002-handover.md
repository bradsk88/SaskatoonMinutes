## What landed this session

**The detail page has three weights instead of one.** On the June 24
council meeting it drew 73 cards for 16 real decisions. Now:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| full cards | 73 | **36** |
| divider rules | — | 11 |
| rows in the routine strip | — | 26 |

- **Routine rows** — call to order, conflict declarations, adjournment,
  and headings standing over nothing — fold into one closed strip at the
  foot, keeping their play buttons. The rule is in
  `mark_row_weights` (`app/agenda_items.py`), called by both producers.
  Evidence for the rule: across **276 meetings**, not one instance of
  those titles has ever had a summary written for it.
- A procedural row with **500+ characters of its own content** stays a
  card. That keeps Question Period (2,089 characters of residents'
  questions) and Council Members (988), and demotes the call to order
  (140) and the adjournment (38). Length is blunt; the two kinds
  separate cleanly at that meeting's numbers.
- **Headings are rules**, not cards. As cards they wore a NOT DISCUSSED
  badge and a topic chip and read like a decision nobody examined.
- **Consent items now show their summary.** One clause,
  `hasChips = !isConsent && ...`, threw away a bullet and chips that had
  already been written and cached — 17 of June 24's rows, including a
  $1.2M loan to TCU Place. The index card had been showing them all along.
- **The header stopped contradicting the page.** It said "43 agenda
  items" above 73 cards. It now reads "19 discussed · 17 approved
  without debate", counted from the list the page renders.

**A speaker row on the card is one line.** The populate run filled in
what each speaker argued and the card took it as three or four more
bullets each, which is what made the index too dense to scan.
`_format_speaker_row` no longer puts remarks on the row at all — the
remarks are still what *earns* it, since only a speaker the transcript
has talking is a candidate. "Spoke to council" went too: directly under
the item they spoke to it said nothing. An orphaned speaker still gets
"Spoke on \<item\>".

**An organization is a coloured chip.** `organization_color` hashes the
name to one of ten palette slots, so Saskatoon Police Service is the
same colour on every page a reader sees it on. Across the real 41
organizations the slots come out evenly used, 2–7 each. Both light and
dark palettes are defined, with a test that fails if a slot is styled in
one theme and not the other.

**A speaker with no organization says "Resident"** — Brad chose the word
over "Citizen" and "Speaking for themselves". Nine of the 72. It is the
quietest chip on the page on purpose.

**Staff are no longer guest speakers.** `_VERB_RE` matches "presented",
which published **32 staff appearances** as guests. Two gates now, in
`app/speakers.py`: the clerk's formula ("presented the report and
responded to questions of the Board"), and an employer test for a City
division or the City itself. Across the archive that drops **21 staff**
and keeps **18 delegates**.

**The organization is the body, not the job.** The prompt asked who a
speaker spoke FOR and never said to drop the title, so the model
answered with the whole self-introduction. Two new rules, plus a
build-time cleanup (`clean_organization`) that fixes what is already
cached without paying for a re-run — five of the 41, e.g. "Executive
Director, The Salvation Army" → "The Salvation Army".

## Traps

- **`_site/` on disk predates the roster shipping.** `merge_remarks`
  returns `[]` against it, so a preview built from the local `_site`
  silently shows no speakers at all. This cost real time. To preview a
  real page: fetch live with `EscribeMeetingSource(LiveEscribeTransport())`
  and pull the summaries with
  `git show origin/summaries:summaries/<id>.json`. Network reaches
  eSCRIBE from here.
- **`hash()` is salted per interpreter.** An organization's colour must
  never come from it, or the Flask app and the static build disagree and
  each build disagrees with the last. `organization_color` uses crc32,
  and a test spawns a second interpreter to hold that.
- **Screenshotting works, with two catches.** `firefox --screenshot
  <absolute path> --window-size=W,H file://...` renders the page's
  JavaScript. Passing `--headless` *first* makes it write nothing. And a
  preview copy moved into a different directory loses its relative
  `style.css` and renders unstyled — which looks like a CSS bug and is
  not one.
- **Video order separates a heading from its items.** They are timed by
  when council discussed them; the heading is timed by its parent
  section. Two rules stacked with nothing between reads as a broken
  page, so a rule over nothing is dropped at render time.
- **A build-time count and a rendered row count will drift** unless the
  same function produces both. That is what "43 agenda items" above 73
  cards was.
- Everything in `001` still applies: the speaker pass must see the
  **whole** transcript slice (a 12,000-character cap reported 1 of 5
  speakers), speech-to-text destroys names so match by organization,
  a registered no-show must come back empty, `git commit` fails on an
  unchanged re-save, `deploy.yml` fires on the summarize run
  **completing** rather than succeeding, `summarize_meetings.py` does not
  load `.env`, the judge is noisy, no `gh` and no GitHub token in the
  session, model is `gemini-2.5-flash`.
